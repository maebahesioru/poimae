About the challenge
Arbiter Hacks V1 runs two parallel
tracks. Pick one when you
register, or switch before the
submission deadline. You may
only submit to one
track.
General Track:
Build anything. Any
language, any stack, any idea. This
track rewards technical ambition and
execution, regardless of subject.
Theme Track, Rethinking Waste:
Build something that helps people or
systems reduce, sort, or recycle waste
more effectively. Examples include a
tool that identifies whether an item is
recyclable, an app that routes food
waste to donation, a hardware or vision
system that sorts materials, or a
dataset or model that improves
recycling accuracy. If it moves the
needle on waste, it fits.
The tracks
are judged separately, against separate
rubrics, for separate prizes. A
polished General submission and a
high-impact Theme submission compete
only within their own track.
Get started
Register and pick a
track.
Form a team of [1 to 4] or go
solo.
Late submissions are
not judged.
Requirements
What to Build
General Track:
Any working software or hardware project. No topic restriction.
Theme Track:
A project whose primary purpose addresses waste reduction, sorting, or recycling. Judges will check that the theme is central to the project, not bolted on. A to-do app with a "recycling" label is a General project, not a Theme one.
What to Submit
Every submission must include:
A public code repository (GitHub, GitLab, or equivalent).
A demo video, [2 to 3] minutes max, showing the project working. Upload to YouTube or Vimeo and link it.
A written description: what it does, how you built it, and what you'd do next.
Theme Track only: one paragraph stating the specific waste problem you targeted and how you'd measure whether it worked.
Missing the repo or the video makes a submission ineligible.

Judging Criteria
Technical Execution (General)
Code quality, working functionality, and the difficulty of what was built. Weighted 35%.
Innovation & Originality
How novel the idea or approach is. Weighted 25%.
Completeness & Polish (General)
How finished the project is. Rough edges, broken features, and unfinished flows lose points. Weighted 25%.
Presentation & Demo Clarity (General)
How clearly the demo video and writeup explain what was built. Weighted 15%.
Impact on Waste Problem (Theme)
How meaningfully the project reduces, sorts, or improves recycling of waste. The theme must be central, not bolted on. Weighted 35%.
Technical Execution (Theme)
Code quality, working functionality, and the difficulty of what was built. Weighted 25%.
Feasibility (Theme)
Could this actually be deployed in the real world? A working solution beats a clever one that can't ship. Weighted 20%.
Presentation & Demo Clarity (Theme)
How clearly the demo video and writeup explain what was built. Weighted 20%.
